About the publication / September 2026

Show the
work.

Infinity is a magazine shaped around work in progress. A striking image can open a story; a clear label tells you what you are looking at.

01

Name the form.

An essay argues a position. A working note describes a question or method. A prototype demonstrates a limited capability. A proposal sets out a possible future. None should be mistaken for a peer reviewed result or an operating institution.

02

Trace the evidence.

Substantive research claims should link to original sources where available, state meaningful uncertainty, and identify what would change the conclusion. Project Atlas records what exists and what remains to be checked. A search link is a starting point for reading, not a citation for a specific claim.

03

Separate image from record.

Editorial illustrations and conceptual architecture express an idea. They are not photographs of a completed product, built campus, or real event. Captions and alternative text should make that distinction clear.

04

Credit and correct.

Outside headlines link to their publisher. Original work is distinguished from collaborative and academic work. Corrections should update the affected page and identify the changed claim. Where a source cannot be cleared for public use, the site should describe the work without publishing the material.
