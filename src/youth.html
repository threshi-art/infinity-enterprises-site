The Infinity Foundation / Helping Youth

A future
worth giving.

Care for children who need stability. An academy built around serious learning and a wider horizon.

The youth vision / In design

Belonging and
possibility.

Two proposed programs sit beneath Helping Youth. The first explores a children's home for young people who need care and support. The second is Pacific Royal Academy, an ambitious boarding and day school for STEM, arts, and civic learning.

Both are concepts. Their design must be tested with educators, child welfare specialists, families, young people, regulators, and a credible funding plan before any service opens.

01 / The orphanage concept

A home before
an institution.

A proposed care initiative centered on safety, consistent relationships, education, and a child's own voice.

Children's home / Concept

Care must be personal.

The foundation would explore residential support for children who cannot safely remain in their current home, while protecting family connections and looking toward stable, lasting care. The exact service model, age range, capacity, and location have not been set.

Design obligations

Build around children.

  • Use qualified child welfare leadership and licensed care pathways.
  • Prioritize safe family and kin connections where appropriate.
  • Provide independent safeguarding, trauma informed support, and meaningful youth participation.
  • Define education, health, transition, and aftercare plans before opening.

01 / Admissions

Rigorous entry.
Human judgment.

The proposed sequence includes a math test, science test, essay, video introduction, parent and student interview, and campus walkthrough. Reaching the interview is intended to signal likely admission, pending parent approval and final review.

02 / Campus & learning

Built for breadth.

The plan calls for AI, robotics, quantum, arts, and athletics complexes; 12 to 14 dormitories; four lecture halls; two dining halls; and a full student union. Boarding, day, and flexible boarding options are proposed.

03 / Faculty & culture

A serious place
to grow.

About 240 to 250 total staff are envisioned, including 41 core academic teachers plus specialist faculty, coaches, boarding, counseling, administration, security, and police roles. Four year faculty contracts, a tenure track, and performance bonuses are proposed.

On the first Sunday of each month, all students would take part in campus beautification, including cleaning, litter pickup, and dorm upkeep. A dean led inspection with security and campus police would review cleanliness, safety, and compliance.

04 / Student life & safety

Worldly. Grounded.
Protected.

Two mandatory annual trips are proposed: Washington, D.C., and a rotating STEM destination such as NASA, a research institution, or a technology campus. These are examples, not announced partnerships.

The safety plan includes a drive through gate pavilion, vehicle scan arch, greeting booth and visitor routing, bag X ray and walk through screening, a security center, CCTV, a drone operations bay, and an on campus police substation. The staffing concept specifies 85 security personnel and 22 nonweaponized drones.

The operating idea

Discipline with
a purpose.

The academy combines demanding academics with arts, athletics, residential life, and campus stewardship. Safety and belonging must be designed together, then assessed against student wellbeing and real operating requirements.

Financial concept

A model to test.

The planning case assumes a $1 billion endowment whose principal remains invested, with a 5% annual distribution assumption. These figures are targets and scenarios, not secured assets, audited forecasts, or a claim of current operations.

At 5%, a $1 billion endowment would contribute an assumed $50 million per year. The remaining $28.8 million in the income model needs identified sources. At a 15 student cap, 3,000 students imply 200 sections if all meet at once; that is a scale check, not a staffing formula. The 41 core teacher figure needs a full timetable, subject load, specialist staffing, and boarding coverage review.

Vision first.
Proof next.

This page is a concept brief. It does not announce an operating school, open admissions, residential placements, funded endowment, or approved nonprofit status. The next work is independent feasibility, child welfare and school licensing review, a complete staffing model, and credible financing.
